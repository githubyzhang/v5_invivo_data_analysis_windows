## Channel 248 / global channel 1921

The full raw scan establishes a source-level anomaly: 1,827,501 / 3,000,000 samples (60.9167%) are exactly zero; all remaining values are negative. The next-highest zero fraction among the other 511 pilot channels is 0.5104%. There are 2,531 constant runs lasting at least 100 samples, with a longest run of 19,208 samples (1.9208 nominal seconds). This 100-sample descriptive inventory is distinct from the historical guard's 10-sample threshold.

The entire b248/global1921 trace exactly matches channel 1921 of the user's `C:\DATA\cnea_data\invivo\26-11-06\0192\extra_4_signals.h5` after float16-to-float32 conversion. Twelve additional 1,000-sample excerpts on three other channels also match. The anomaly therefore predates Windows export; this bounded comparison does not establish the full HDF5 file identity or identify whether acquisition, conversion, quantization or another process caused the pattern. No calibrated ADC rail is inferred.

Cluster 36 has its whitened-template peak on b248. It has 339 events; 334 occur at negative, nonzero b248 samples and five at zero samples. Its selected raw mean is embedded in a broad shared baseline slope, and representative uncentered traces show a large offset between b248 and adjacent channels. This warrants a hold on single-unit acceptance; it does not prove all its events are false or that channel removal will improve the sort.

## The earlier per-channel clipping guard

The user directed this review to the existing feature. [METHODS](../METHODS.md#quality-and-observability) and `analysis/pilot.py` define it as exact repeated stored values for **at least 10 samples**, expanded **500 samples on each side**. `analysis/detect_recordings.py` excludes those per-channel samples from detector noise estimation and candidate detection. The guard is a clipping proxy and cannot reconstruct lost information. It is separate from shared-disturbance warnings and from a whole-channel exclusion.

The frozen pilot already contains `outputs/preflight/prior_flat_guards.csv`: 633 intervals across the mapped shank. The [guard replay](../../results/extra4_shank0/pilot01-round2-reviewed/clipping-guard/summary.json) uses the actual inherited helpers on full raw traces for 18 channels (reviewed peaks plus immediate b248 neighbors), and matches the frozen masks exactly on every checked channel. A separate interval-union calculation also agrees. Other channels' coverage is taken from the frozen intervals, not represented as freshly replayed.

For b248, the historical guard comprises 94 merged intervals covering 2,392,588 samples (**79.7529%**, leaving 60.7412 nominal / 60.2591 physical seconds outside this guard). Cluster 36 has **131/339 event centers** and **132/339 complete-or-clipped [-20,+40] supports** intersecting its peak-channel guard. These annotations do not censor the events. Other units' peak-channel overlap is tabulated in the packet; peak-channel-only overlap is not a validated unit-observability rule because Kilosort uses multiple channels and temporal/spatial preprocessing.

**Proposed next work:** define and validate a Kilosort-specific observability policy before accepting units or expanding recordings. Start from the historical guard and preserve its sample coordinates/provenance; examine relevant multichannel support and preprocessing effects. Do not automatically apply the union of all per-channel guards to the whole shank, zero-fill raw samples, or divide unit counts by a single electrode's remaining time. A separately named bad-channel sensitivity comparison is now justified as a hypothesis, but is not launched or adopted: isolate channel selection from the FAISS change using matched runs in the repaired environment, and assess waveform quality/stability and event correspondence rather than maximizing unit counts. Root cause of the source anomaly remains open.
